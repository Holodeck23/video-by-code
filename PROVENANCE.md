# Provenance and reuse

The kit is MIT-licensed (`LICENSE`). It synthesises engineering and direction techniques from the owner's code-driven
video experiments: music/lyric pipelines, instrumental studies, spoken explainers,
short-form experiments and reference-led motion tests. It is written around technique
rather than project histories or private chats.

## Source classes

| Material | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Owner's render/timing/verification experiments | Adapted engineering lessons; APIs unified into the kit contract |
| Saved extraction reports (2026-11-04) | Research input, including reported failures and acceptance limits |
| Futurepedia Style Atlas 86 prompt exports | Visual-technique reference; rewritten style specifications and briefs |
| External framework documentation | Linked primary sources in the relevant chapters; implementations are optional |
| Nick Saraev's free Maker Zero anime-explainer skill pack (2026-11) | Method reference for chapter 13; techniques re-described in new wording, `mouth-track.py` written independently, 8-word overlap check passes against the pack's docs and code. No file from the pack is distributed |
| New examples, templates and packaging | Original teaching and handoff material for this kit |

The two extraction reports remain in the author's private research archive outside this
repository. Their benchmarks describe particular versions on particular hardware. They
are not throughput guarantees or proof of creative acceptance.

The original Style Atlas archive remains outside this kit. No original prompt export,
poster code or media is distributed in the kit. The cards describe physical appearance,
mark-making, motion, sound and scene construction in newly authored wording. These are
specifications a team must implement and inspect. A short substring-overlap checker
detects shared runs; it does not establish legal clearance or independent expression by
itself. Rendered studies are diagnostic samples, not certification of all 86 styles.

Some engineering ideas in the owner's history came through third-party engines or
references. Where kit code adapts an MIT-licensed implementation, the file is listed in
`THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` with the original licence. If a future production copies another
implementation, preserve its source attribution and applicable licence. Framework,
font, music and model terms must be checked for that production. This repository does
not invent a new licence for externally owned material or grant rights to reference art.

Creative quality and technical correctness remain distinct. Source logs containing
rejected drafts, prototypes or unreviewed renders are evidence of experiments, not of
accepted films. No style card implies the owner approved a finished production in it.
