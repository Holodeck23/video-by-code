# Lyric or music-led film brief

Work from the selected audio take, not the generation prompt. Confirm the intended song version, approved lyric text, aspect ratio and visual direction. Save the audio hash and probe its duration before analysis; all timing files must identify the same source.

Measure structure, beats, instrumental breaks and lyric phrases from the recording. A requested drop, stop or silence may be absent in the final take. Mark each intended event as located, approximate or absent. Do not manufacture a timed event just because it appears in the song prompt.

Treat embedded lyric cues as provisional phrase anchors. Check their starts and ends by listening; they may span long instrumental gaps. Word alignment needs known text plus the actual vocal recording, with low-confidence or interpolated intervals explicitly labelled.

Build a song map, then choose a small number of visual motifs that develop across sections. A returning chorus should feel related while gaining a new scale, material, consequence or viewpoint. Avoid generic particles and waveform decoration as the sole visual argument.

The selected style may govern typography and diagrams, while a separate art direction defines the song's subject. Prove three representative stills and a 10-20-second pilot before investing in a full track. Human approval of those images is a separate gate from passing tests.

Render frames from absolute song time. For audio-driven motion, sample precomputed features or use measured event times; never depend on a live analyser during export. Distinguish beat pulses, phrase accents and section-level transformations so they do not all fire together.

Lyrics must remain readable: phrase groups, intentional emphasis and holds through the end of the sung word. Never stretch an early first-word onset across silence. If alignment is uncertain, prefer line-level timing and state the limitation.

Use a deterministic page contract, seeded textures and seek-safe effects. Render clip audio from the same --from timestamp as the picture. The music recording is the master track; optional effects must be mixed deliberately and kept out of the way of vocals.

Verify full decode, exact frames, audio duration, colour and source identity. Sample boundaries, high-motion sections and fine saturated lines after encoding. Watch/listen through for cut harshness, strobing, repeated imagery and late lyrics; frame counts cannot catch those problems.

Deliver code, source identity, section map, verified/approximate lyric timing, pilot and final render, contact sheet and production log. Keep source audio and renders outside the code tree. Never label unreviewed lyric alignment or visual style as approved.
